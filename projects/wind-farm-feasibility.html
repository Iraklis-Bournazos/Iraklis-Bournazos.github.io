Wind farm feasibility study, Skärvagshållan

Technical and economic assessment of an 80 MW wind farm in mountainous central Sweden.

MATLABPythonJensen wake modelLCOE / NPVBESS sizing
Read the full report (PDF)Code on GitHub

Scope

An 80 MW onshore wind farm at Skärvagshållan, a mountainous site in central Sweden inside bidding zone SE3. The question was whether the project should be built.

What I investigated

  • Turbine technology — the four generator concepts, then wind class, cold-climate package, turbulence and transport into complex terrain
  • Layout and wake losses — modelled with the Jensen wake model, validated against the Renewables.ninja benchmark
  • Grid connection — nine candidate buses, voltage rise, reactive power capability
  • Economics — NPV and LCOE across the investment-cost range over a 25-year lifetime
  • Storage — hybrid BESS sizing against SE3 prices under a constrained export connection

What came out

Vestas V136 full converter, two-row staggered layout. The ridge-aligned arc at 7D spacing yields more energy, but needs 13.9 × 12.8 km — more than the site allows, and at 4D its advantage disappears.

Grid connection decides more than cost. Ignoring line impedance, all nine buses accept 80 MW and the 10 kV node looks cheapest. Including it, voltage rise makes 10 kV and 20 kV infeasible; only the 70 kV side of the 20/70 kV transformer stays inside grid-code limits.

Profitability turns on the investment cost, not the wind resource: NPV +109 MSEK at 0.852 BSEK (LCOE 361.6 SEK/MWh), −622 MSEK at 1.58 BSEK (573.2 SEK/MWh).

A 45 MWh battery raises annual net revenue by about 4.6% by recovering curtailed energy. 133 MWh shows clear diminishing returns.
